# Writing as Design Material

Copy is design intent, not decoration. Bring the same intentionality to words that you bring to spacing and color.

## Core Principle

Words appear in a design for one reason: to make it easier to understand, and therefore easier to use. Before writing anything, ask what the design needs to say, and how it can best be said to help the person navigate the experience.

## Active Voice as Default

A control should say exactly what happens when it's used: "Save changes," not "Submit." An action keeps the same name through the whole flow — the button that says "Publish" produces a toast that says "Published." Cohesion and consistency are how people learn their way around.

## Write from the User's Side

Name things by what people control and recognize, never by how the system is built. A person manages notifications, not webhook config. Describe what something does in plain terms rather than selling it. Being specific is always better than being clever.

## Specificity Over Cleverness

Generic copy makes a design feel as templated as generic visuals. Examples:

| Generic (avoid) | Specific (prefer) |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| "Get started today" | "Add your first project" |
| "Something went wrong" | "Couldn't save — check your connection and try again" |
| "Learn more" | "See how pricing works" |
| "Welcome back!" | "3 tasks due today" |

## Failure and Empty States

Treat failure and emptiness as moments for direction, not mood. Explain what went wrong and how to fix it, in the interface's voice. Errors don't apologize, and they are never vague about what happened. An empty screen is an invitation to act — tell the user exactly what to do next.

## Register and Tone

Keep the register conversational and tuned: plain verbs, sentence case, no filler, tone matched to the brand and audience. Let each element do exactly one job — a label labels, an example demonstrates, nothing quietly does double duty.

## In Design Briefs Without Real Content

When a brief has no real copy, write placeholder text that fits the subject's world — not "Lorem ipsum," not "Title goes here." Invent realistic content that proves the design works with actual words at actual lengths. Generic placeholder copy is a warning sign that the design only works in an idealized vacuum.
